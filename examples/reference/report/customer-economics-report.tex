\SectionDivider{06}{Model-based forecasting of cohort trajectories}{The renewal and revenue mechanisms that extend incomplete cohort histories to a common decision horizon.}
\PageTitle{06 / MODEL MECHANISM}{How membership histories become cohort forecasts}{Hierarchical shifted-Beta-Geometric retention + Gamma gross revenue; NumPyro NUTS}
\Section{The renewal mechanism}
The shifted-Beta-Geometric mechanism follows \SourceCite{fader2007}{Fader and Hardie (2007)}. Each member has a persistent propensity to leave at a monthly renewal. Within a joining-cohort, channel and stated-need cell $g$, the model represents that heterogeneity with a Beta distribution. Members with higher churn propensity tend to leave earlier, so the surviving group's average churn falls even when no individual's underlying propensity changes.
\[
p_{i,g}\sim\operatorname{Beta}(\alpha_g,\beta_g),\qquad
h_g(t)=\frac{\alpha_g}{\alpha_g+\beta_g+t-1},\qquad t\geq1.
\]
Here $p_{i,g}$ is member $i$'s latent monthly churn propensity and $h_g(t)$ is churn among members who reach renewal $t$. The first paid receipt is age zero; it establishes the acquired population and supplies no renewal trial. The count of customers who renew is modeled from the previous month's survivors:
\[
N_{g,t}\mid N_{g,t-1}\sim\operatorname{Binomial}\!\left(N_{g,t-1},1-h_g(t)\right).
\]
\Section{What differs between customer groups}
The mean of the Beta distribution varies with baseline need, acquisition channel and joining-month trend, plus a partially pooled cohort effect. Its concentration varies with need and channel. Pooling lets a young cohort borrow evidence from older cohorts while allowing observed cohort differences. The model uses these baseline attributes; later activity and feedback are diagnostic evidence, rather than forecasting inputs.
\Section{Revenue and contribution}
A Gamma model adapted from \SourceCite{orduz2024}{Orduz (2024)} represents payment revenue. Its expected total is the number of retained members multiplied by expected spending per retained member:
\[
\operatorname{E}[R_{g,a}\mid N_{g,a}]=N_{g,a}\,r_{g,a}.
\]
Expected spending $r_{g,a}$ varies with customer age, joining month, need and channel. The payment target is after discounts and before refunds. Observed refund and variable-cost ratios translate future payment revenue into contribution. Actual contribution is kept wherever it is observed; only the unobserved remainder is forecast.
\Section{Estimation and scope}
NumPyro NUTS estimates the model from completed renewal and payment histories on CPU. Pending decisions and future ages remain unobserved. Cancellation is absorbing in this business. Forecasts start from the last actual survivor count and cover each cohort's first twelve monthly receipts. The appendix reports priors, parameter estimates and chronological validation; the separate Orduz-inspired neural model supplies a comparison, not executive LTV.
\Boundary{The bands describe posterior parameter uncertainty conditional on the fitted model and fixed economic ratios. They exclude future customer-level variation, uncertain costs and business-regime changes. With no calendar-time term, a common disruption can affect multiple cohorts outside the displayed bands.}

\SectionDivider{07}{Customer value forecasts}{Contribution valuation, acquisition-segment strategy and the assumptions behind immature-cohort LTV.}
\PageTitle{07 / CONTRIBUTION VALUE}{A common horizon connects\newline renewal to acquisition economics}{Twelve monthly receipts | Observed contribution is retained; only the unobserved remainder is forecast.}
\Section{From the forecast path to value}
The model explained in Section 6 supplies the expected contribution path, preserving the retention heterogeneity emphasized by \SourceCite{fader2010}{Fader and Hardie (2010)}. Discount each receipt, sum twelve receipts, then subtract the acquisition cost per original customer. The first paid receipt is age zero and is discounted by one month. Cash payback instead uses the first undiscounted crossing of cumulative contribution over CAC.
\[\operatorname{net\,LTV}_{H}=\sum_{a=0}^{H-1}\frac{\operatorname{E}[\text{contribution}_{a}\mid\text{observed history}]}{(1+d)^{a+1}}-\operatorname{CAC}.\]
\Evidence{A separate revenue model estimates spending per retained member. Observed revenue and contribution are preserved; only the remaining receipts are forecast, with recorded cost and refund relationships held fixed. Values cover each cohort's first twelve monthly receipts, discounted at 1\% per month. The model has no calendar-time effect: common disruptions can move cohorts together and are outside these parameter bands. The horizon covers twelve receipts, not an infinite lifetime. Bands describe uncertainty in expected value conditional on the model.}

\Section{Likelihood, heterogeneity and economic conversion}
Each member has a persistent churn propensity. Within a cohort/need/channel cell, propensities follow one Beta distribution. After t-1 renewals, churn hazard is $\alpha/(\alpha+\beta+t-1)$; observed survivors follow a Binomial with the previous survivor count as its risk set. Mean propensity varies with need, channel, cohort trend and a partially pooled cohort effect. Concentration varies with need and channel. Variance is $\mu(1-\mu)/(\phi+1)$. Falling aggregate churn can reflect survivor selection, not improvement in each individual's loyalty. Gross payment revenue follows a Gamma distribution with shape proportional to observed retained members. Expected revenue per retained member varies with log age, cohort trend, need and channel. Observed age-aligned net and contribution shares translate future gross revenue into economics. Pending renewals and future ages are unobserved, not failures. Age zero establishes acquisition and supplies no renewal trial. Churn is absorbing in this business.
\[\begin{aligned}p_{i,g}&\sim\operatorname{Beta}(\alpha_g,\beta_g), & \alpha_g&=\mu_g\phi_g, &\beta_g&=(1-\mu_g)\phi_g,\\N_{g,t}\mid N_{g,t-1}&\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(N_{g,t-1},1-h_g(t)), & h_g(t)&=\frac{\alpha_g}{\alpha_g+\beta_g+t-1}.\end{aligned}\]

\Section{Feature transformations and interpretation}

\Section{Method sources and adaptation}
\Evidence{Retention extends Fader-Hardie sBG with mean/concentration parameterization, logit/log covariate links, a non-centered cohort effect on mean churn, grouped conditional Binomial renewal likelihood and NUTS estimation. Revenue adapts Orduz's Gamma structure but uses observed active counts in fitting, an additional per-member shape multiplier, a mean link without an age-by-cohort interaction, need/channel covariates and \$100 scaling. Conditional on observed activity, the revenue and retention likelihoods have independent parameter blocks. Observed contribution is copied identically into the actual and predicted totals. The forecast covers only the remaining months. The naive baseline freezes the last three observed renewal rates and revenue per retained customer. The December row makes this low-information comparison visible. The baseline underpredicts all nine rows with at least three forecast receipts; its tendency to underpredict increases the apparent advantage of the fitted model.
\Section{What the payback forecast represents}
Forecast payback is the first crossing of acquisition cost by the \textbf{posterior-mean expected contribution path}. Posterior parameter bands also exclude future customer-level process noise.

\Section{What adjustment does and does not resolve}
Year--channel--need standardization for event groups leaves within-year cohort differences. Survey contrasts instead align joining month, channel and need, so months without a recorded response cannot enter the comparison. Sparse survey groups lose coverage; results apply to supported cells. Neither adjustment resolves plan selection, unmeasured experience or selective survey participation. No recorded response retains unknown invitation status.
\Boundary{The figures and tables describe associations in the released observation window. They do not establish a friction effect, an individual targeting rule or prediction on future cohorts. A randomized intervention with a protected control is needed to estimate whether reducing a specific friction changes retention and contribution.}
