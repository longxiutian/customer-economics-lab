# Methodological and instructional sources

The lab's synthetic records and generator are original. These references explain the teaching ideas; their datasets, graphics, articles, and videos are not included as redistributable lab data.

- **Gil Bouhnick — Cohort analysis and retention rate.** [Mobilespoon article](https://www.mobilespoon.net/2019/07/cohort-analysis-retention-rate.html). Inspiration for comparing customers, orders, revenue, cumulative value, and acquisition recovery across joining cohorts. The lab computes its own contribution from explicit revenue and variable-cost records; a transaction commission is not silently treated as a complete margin.
- **Peter S. Fader and Bruce G. S. Hardie (2007), “How to Project Customer Retention,” Journal of Interactive Marketing, 21(1), 76–90.** [Published article](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1002/dir.20074); [author-hosted working paper](https://www.brucehardie.com/papers/021/) (May 2006 version). This is the main shifted-Beta-Geometric source. The lab extends its persistent individual dropout mechanism with observed group covariates and partially pooled acquisition-cohort effects; those hierarchical choices are lab adaptations.
- **Peter S. Fader and Bruce G. S. Hardie (2010), “Customer-Base Valuation in a Contractual Setting: The Perils of Ignoring Heterogeneity,” Marketing Science, 29(1), 85–93.** [Author-hosted article and technical appendix](https://www.brucehardie.com/papers/022/), DOI 10.1287/mksc.1080.0482. This supplies the valuation rationale for preserving heterogeneity and survivor selection instead of extrapolating one aggregate retention rate. It does not establish the value of an untested retention intervention in Studio North.
- **Daniel M. McCarthy, Peter S. Fader and Bruce G. S. Hardie (2017), “Valuing Subscription-Based Businesses Using Publicly Disclosed Customer Data,” Journal of Marketing, 81(1), 17–35.** [Published article](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jm.15.0519). This connects customer acquisition, retention and spending to subscription-business valuation. The lab adopts that managerial decomposition, but does not replicate its full corporate-valuation model, fit publicly disclosed company data, or estimate enterprise value. Its decision quantities are twelve-month contribution value, tracked CAC and cash payback.
- **Juan Orduz — Cohort Revenue Retention Analysis with Flax and NumPyro.** [Walkthrough](https://juanitorduz.github.io/revenue_retention_numpyro/). The main model adapts its Gamma revenue structure. A separate four-hidden-unit Bayesian neural network is a retention validation comparison. The benchmark uses conditional renewal risk sets instead of independent cumulative active counts, and an equivalent native JAX network rather than a Flax wrapper. Neither is described as a verbatim reproduction. See [model specification](model-specification.md) and [benchmark provenance](orduz-benchmark.md).
- **Dan McCarthy — Estimating cohort-level CLV from transaction data.** [Video tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UJ6Rfmk5Vrw). Background on transforming transaction histories into age-aligned cohort data and customer-value estimates. This lab uses the term **LTV**; the source video's original title retains CLV.

Supporting software is listed in `pyproject.toml` and `uv.lock`. Archify is bundled separately under its own license; see its vendor directory. The references above do not imply that their authors endorse this lab or its synthetic conclusions.
